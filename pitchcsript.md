# UCIP Pitch Script

This script is written for practice. It follows the 11-slide deck and keeps the tone natural, clear, and confident.

## Suggested delivery

- Target time: 4 to 6 minutes
- Pace: calm, deliberate, not rushed
- Goal: sound like you understand the problem deeply and the solution practically

---

## Slide 1 - The Wake-Up Call

"Good day everyone. Imagine a city of over 22 million people facing heat, flooding, and environmental loss, but making decisions almost completely blind.

Lagos hit 36 degrees this Harmattan. Flooding keeps disrupting places like Oshodi market. Areas like Mushin are losing tree cover. And the biggest issue is this: people do not see these risks early enough, and even when signs exist, nobody is clearly told what to do next.

That is the problem we are solving."

---

## Slide 2 - The Problem

"Today, climate information in Lagos is too broad to be useful at neighbourhood level.

Weather alerts are usually city-wide, not street by street. Heat islands are not tracked at the level where families actually live. Flood warnings are not granular enough. Green-space loss is often tracked too slowly. And many vulnerable people are excluded completely because they use feature phones, not smartphones.

So residents make daily choices without local climate awareness, and planners spend large budgets without precise data or a costed action plan. The result is slow response, wasted resources, and preventable harm."

---

## Slide 3 - Our Solution

"Our solution is UCIP, the Urban Climate Intelligence Platform.

UCIP combines three key layers: citizen reports, satellite-based climate baselines, and live weather data. From that, we generate neighbourhood-level heat insights, flood-risk alerts, green-space monitoring, and most importantly, a solutions engine.

What makes UCIP different is that we do not stop at telling people that risk is high. We go one step further and say: here is what you can do now, here is what government can do next, and here is what each action costs.

And we designed it for inclusion. Smartphone users can access the web app, while feature-phone users can still participate through USSD."

---

## Slide 4 - How It Works

"Here is how the system works.

First, a resident sends a report through the app or USSD. That report is location-tagged. Next, UCIP fuses that report with satellite baselines and live weather data. Then our scoring engine updates risk levels for heat, flood, and vegetation stress.

Those scores then power four outputs: the live map, alerts, the planner dashboard, and the solutions engine.

So the loop is simple: report, fuse, show, alert, and solve. That means our system does not just collect data. It turns local signals into immediate local action."

---

## Slide 5 - Under the Hood

"Technically, UCIP is built to be practical and scalable.

On the frontend, we use React, Vite, Tailwind, and Leaflet. On the backend, we use Node, Express, and spatial storage with PostgreSQL and PostGIS. We also connect weather feeds, satellite baselines, and communication tools like SMS, push notifications, and USSD.

Our scoring is also transparent. For example, heat risk is not guessed. It is calculated from land-surface temperature, citizen severity reports, and live weather temperature.

That matters because we want trust. If a planner or partner asks how a score was produced, we can explain it clearly."

---

## Slide 6 - The Solutions Engine

"This is the part that makes UCIP especially powerful.

Most climate dashboards stop at awareness. UCIP adds prescription.

For every neighbourhood, the platform can generate ranked actions for two groups: what the community can do immediately, and what government should do in the short term.

For example, in a high-heat, low-greenery area like Mushin, UCIP can recommend actions such as cool-roof programmes, community cooling hubs, drain clearance schedules, street tree corridors, or drainage upgrades, each with estimated cost and measurable outcomes.

So instead of just seeing a red zone on a map, decision-makers see a response plan."

---

## Slide 7 - What We’re Building

"We are not presenting just an idea. We already have a working prototype.

The current system already supports the main loop from report to score to map to alert to solution. We have live weather ingestion, a tested USSD flow, a spatial database foundation, and a seeded solutions engine with practical interventions.

What comes next is strengthening production readiness: improving live satellite processing, expanding messaging infrastructure, and piloting with real neighbourhood partners.

So this is not theory. It is already taking shape as a usable system."

---

## Slide 8 - The Market

"The need for UCIP is large and immediate.

Our first market is Lagos residents who need local climate awareness. But the platform is also valuable to urban planners, health agencies, NGOs, civil society groups, and even insurers or property actors who need neighbourhood-level risk intelligence.

Our entry point is Lagos, but the model scales naturally. Once the infrastructure is in place, the same approach can extend across other Nigerian cities and eventually across West Africa.

So UCIP begins as a Lagos solution, but it is designed as a scalable climate intelligence system."

---

## Slide 9 - Impact Vision

"In the next 12 months, success for us means more than user growth.

It means expanding to more neighbourhoods, reaching thousands of residents, especially feature-phone users, integrating stronger satellite processing, and most importantly, seeing real interventions happen because of the platform.

We want to see communities act earlier, governments budget smarter, and climate response become more targeted, more inclusive, and more measurable.

Our vision is simple: climate intelligence for everyone, not just people with smartphones, data plans, or technical expertise."

---

## Slide 10 - What We’re Asking For

"Today, we are asking for more than applause.

First, we are asking for your support and confidence in the solution.

Second, we are asking for strategic connections to government agencies, NGOs, GIS partners, and climate actors who can help us validate and deploy this in real settings.

Third, we are asking for honest feedback. What would make a planner trust this enough to use it in a real budget or intervention decision?

That feedback is critical because our goal is not just to build something impressive. It is to build something used."

---

## Slide 11 - Thank You

"In closing, Lagos has over 22 million people, and climate risk is already shaping how they live, move, work, and survive.

UCIP is our answer: one platform that helps the city see risk earlier, respond faster, and act smarter.

Thank you very much."

---

## Practice tips

- Do not try to memorize every sentence word for word. Memorize the flow of ideas.
- Emphasize these phrases: `neighbourhood-level`, `every phone`, `not just data, but action`, and `climate intelligence for everyone`.
- Pause briefly after Slide 1, Slide 3, and Slide 6. Those are your strongest moments.
- If time is short, compress Slides 5, 7, and 8 first.
